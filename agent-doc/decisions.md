# Sales Call Tracker - Decision Log

- **Decision 1:** Expired bookings are freed by an hourly background job, not by serving. An APScheduler job runs inside the Flask server process: it fires once the moment the server starts, then every hour, and clears `scheduled_by` and `scheduled_at` on every `waiting` lead whose `scheduled_at` sits more than `SCHEDULE_EXPIRE_HOURS` (24) hours in the past — attempt counts untouched, freed leads return to the newest-first pool. The job is the only place that releases expired bookings: serving a lead never frees them, so a lead whose booking just expired waits invisible in the pool for at most one hour, and the manager's Scheduled count drains within the hour. The startup run recovers a server that was asleep or down — everything that expired while it was away is freed within seconds of waking. Duplicate runs are harmless — the release is one idempotent update. The scheduler never starts under tests.

- **Decision 2:** The calls-by-hour charts show their split in dots and center their counts. On the salesperson's My Stats hour chart, the outgoing and incoming counts sit in the vertical middle of their own colored segments — the total stays on top of the bar, and a count appears only when its segment holds at least one eighth of the tallest bar's calls. Both hour charts — web and app — carry a one-row color legend: blue dot = outgoing, green dot = incoming, the same colors the Call Split cards already use. The web keeps its summary line under the legend; the app shows the legend under the bars. The Call Split cards themselves are untouched.

- **Decision 3:** Every call leaves the phone on one chosen number. Settings gains a fourth row listing the phone's readable SIM numbers — each with its carrier name — plus one "Use phone default" item; picking a number saves it on the phone only (international format, never synced). Until a number is picked, calls use Android's default SIM. After a pick, every dial reads the live SIM numbers, matches both sides after normalizing formats (+91 vs 0), and places the call on the matching slot with no SIM picker ever — the call is recorded only after the SIM is found. A stored number that is no longer in the phone blocks the call with "This SIM number is not in the phone." — nothing is dialed and nothing is recorded; a fresh pick in Settings is the fix. Reading the SIM numbers and placing the call runs through one small custom Kotlin module, because no maintained React Native library covers it. Phones below Android 8 stay on the default path. Some OEM skins (OPPO/ColorOS) may flash their own confirm screen; the call still goes out on the chosen SIM — accepted.

- **Decision 4:** The reminder notification carries the app icon. The status bar shows a dedicated white phone shape on a transparent background as the small icon (`ic_reminder_icon`, generated at all five densities) — Android flattens any colored icon to plain white there, so the normal app icon cannot be used. In the notification drawer, the full-color app icon shows as the large icon. Nothing else about the notification changes.

- **Decision 5:** The Calling SIM choice runs on slots, not numbers. Real phones and carriers often leave the SIM's own number unreadable (Android 10+ hides it without READ_PHONE_NUMBERS, and many carriers never store it on the SIM at all), so the picker lists slots — "SIM 1 · Jio", "SIM 2 · Airtel" — with the number shown only when the OS has one. The choice saves as the slot, and dialing places every call on the saved slot through the SIM module; no number matching ever runs. READ_PHONE_NUMBERS stays as a display-only permission, asked once at the dialer's login permission pass alongside the tracking permissions — it never blocks anything, and its denial shows no banner. The Calling SIM screen itself asks no permissions; with the Phone permission off it shows a note and stays on phone default. This supersedes the number-matching parts of Decision 3: the choice follows the slot (SIMs swapped between slots keep the choice on the slot), the blocked-call error is "Could not place the call on this SIM.", and the old stored number choice is dropped on update — the salesperson picks the SIM once more.

- **Decision 6:** The call history lives on the phone, in its own tab. The tab bar grows to five tabs — Dialer, Scheduled, History, My Stats, Settings. History holds every call the phone records, outgoing and incoming, in a new local SQLite table written once per call at the moment the call's real time and duration are known — before any sync, so the list works offline and queue cleanup never touches it. The first open after the update backfills the table once from the phone's own call log, and Do-not-track numbers never get a row — the same rule tracking follows. Every History tab open runs the call-log sweep and saves the last-loaded time on the phone, so calls made while the app was closed show up at the next look. The list reads newest first, 50 rows per page with a Load-more button, and keeps at most 1000 rows — row 1001 pushes the oldest out. A row shows the number, an out or in mark, the date-time, and the result — connected with talk time, or not connected — and carries a Copy button, a WhatsApp button, and a Scheduled badge when the number has an active booking in the local reminders table. No calls yet says "No calls yet."

- **Decision 7:** Every booking stays visible until it is handled. The Scheduled tab reads the local `scheduled_reminders` table that already stores each booking's number and time, and lists every booking — lead bookings and keypad bookings alike — with the number, the booked date and time, and a state: Upcoming while the booked time has not come, Due once it has passed with no tap and no call. Due rows sort first, most overdue at top, then Upcoming rows soonest first. A row carries Copy and WhatsApp buttons, and tapping it opens the booked lead's card ready to call — the same thing tapping the notification does. The row leaves the list when the notification is tapped, or a call to that number is recorded, or 24 hours pass its booked time — the same window the server uses to free the lead; the cleanup runs at every app open. Every app open counts the Due rows and shows the count as a badge on the Scheduled tab, and the tab shows the line "You have N leads you have not called." The tab is fully local and works offline; nothing booked says "Nothing scheduled."

- **Decision 8:** WhatsApp opens on the chosen app only. Settings gains two rows saved on the phone only: the WhatsApp app row holds WhatsApp or WhatsApp Business with WhatsApp preselected, and every WhatsApp button in the app opens only the chosen one; the First message row holds a switch, off by default, plus a text field — off opens the chat empty, on opens the chat with that text already typed, and WhatsApp always lets the salesperson edit before sending, so nothing ever sends by itself. Opening runs through a small Kotlin module beside the SIM module: it launches the chosen app by its exact package — WhatsApp `com.whatsapp`, Business `com.whatsapp.w4b` — with the number in international format and the optional text, and Android shows the existing chat with that number or a fresh one. If the chosen app is not installed, a toast says "WhatsApp is not installed." and nothing else happens.

- **Decision 9:** The keypad clears itself, and the lead card hands out its number. On the keypad, submitting the post-call popup — "Do not schedule" or "Schedule" alike — clears the typed number to blank, leaving the display empty for the next number; paste, backspace, and hold-to-clear stay as they are. The automated flow keeps its number and its popup untouched — its number comes from the lead pool, not typed. The automated lead calling card gains a Copy button next to the lead's number: one tap copies it with a "Number copied." toast, available while ringing, during the call, and on the post-call popup, so a salesperson who must dial elsewhere goes copy, paste, dial straight from the card.

- **Decision 10:** The app catches phone numbers tapped anywhere on the phone. One Android intent filter on the app's main activity answers `tel:` dial links, so tapping a number in Contacts, WhatsApp, a browser, or SMS shows Android's chooser with our app among the choices, and choosing Always hands our app every tapped number from then on. The app never becomes the default phone app — incoming call screens, the in-call screen, and the home-screen dial button all stay Android's. A caught number lands on the Dialer tab already typed in the keypad with the Call button ready, and the call goes out through the same keypad flow: on the chosen SIM, recorded like every call, with the normal post-call popup; backing out leaves the number in the keypad and nothing happens. The link's number is cleaned before it fills the keypad — spaces and dashes stripped, plus and digits kept; an empty or unreadable link just opens the dialer with nothing filled. While the automated lead call flow is on screen a caught number is ignored — the live flow is never disturbed, and the lead card's Copy button remains the way to dial elsewhere. A Do-not-track number dialed this way still places the call and is never recorded — the same rule every call follows; a call with no SIM picked still leaves on the phone default. The login permission pass gains one step in the same step-by-step style: it checks quietly which app handles tapped numbers — ours means skip silently — and otherwise shows one short instruction line and fires Android's chooser once, the probe number never dialed; skipping brings the step back at the next login until picked. The step carries a "Don't show again" button found only there: tapping it saves the refusal on the phone permanently and the step never appears again, even when the app is never picked.
